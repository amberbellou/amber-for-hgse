How HGSE Actually Works

Most candidates promise to be your advocate. Almost none can tell you which room the decision gets made in. This is that map, and exactly what I would do in each room.

For: conversations with students, week of September 8Voting: September 8–11, ballot by HGSE email

Part one

The rooms where things get decided

HGSE does not have one student government that runs everything. It has several bodies, each with a different job and a different kind of power. Knowing which is which is the difference between a complaint and a change.

Student Advisory BoardsSAB · one per program

Each master's program has its own board. Members are the liaison between students in that program and HGSE faculty and administration, and they help shape program events and initiatives.

Membership is handled at the program level, so it varies by program. Doctoral representation from Ph.D. and Ed.L.D. students runs through the Director for Doctoral Studies.

Convening of Student Program RepresentativesCSPR · once a semester

All program board members meet together, hosted by the Office of Student Affairs, and senior administrators attend. This is where issues affecting the whole school get raised, not just one program.

The part almost nobody knows: representatives submit agenda items to the Office of Student Affairs in advance. The agenda is not handed down. It is built from what representatives send in.

This is the single biggest lever a representative has

Student CouncilRepresentatives by degree program

Made up of representatives from each master's and doctoral program. It works through committees, including the Student Advocacy and Issues Committee, whose written job is to find out what students actually think about academic, social and community matters, and then take that to faculty, staff and other decision-makers.

The same committee is charged with working across schools, not only inside HGSE.

Diversity and Inclusion Advisory CouncilDIAC · five times a semester

Elected students from program cohorts and affinity organizations sit alongside the Associate Dean for Enrollment and Student Services, the Director of Student Affairs, and the Assistant Director for Multicultural and International Affairs.

DIAC controls real money. It reviews proposals to the Dean's Diversity Innovation Fund and decides which student initiatives get funded.

Harvard Graduate CouncilHGC · all twelve schools

The university-wide student government for every Harvard graduate and professional school. HGSE sends one voting representative, who is expected to gather input from HGSE students before voting.

HGC exists to break down the walls between the twelve schools, and it administers funding for cross-school activity, including a fund that gives student groups $500 or $2,500 for events that bring schools together. Very few HGSE groups ever apply.

So the honest answer to “can a student rep actually change anything?” is this: a rep cannot set tuition, admissions or grades. A rep can put an item on the agenda that senior administrators sit down to discuss, decide where diversity funding goes, and unlock cross-school money almost nobody claims.

Those are small levers. Pulled deliberately, with evidence, they move real things.

Part three

The first ninety days

In a one-year program there is no time for a slow start. This is the order I would work in.

Keep collecting, in the open

The site is already live and already receiving concerns, anonymously if people prefer. It keeps running. Volume matters, because ten students saying the same thing is evidence and one student saying it is an anecdote.

Already running

Turn what students said into agenda items

Sort every response by topic, count them, and submit the top items to the Office of Student Affairs ahead of the semester's convening. Walk in with numbers instead of impressions.

Before the convening

Meet the three people who can actually unblock things

The Assistant Dean of Student Affairs, the staff member responsible for community building and international student support, and the administrator who handles student organization funding. Ask each what they need from students to say yes.

First month

Ship two visible wins early

The student-faculty mixer and the shared opportunities board. Both are cheap, both were asked for by students, and both prove the channel works so more people use it.

By November

Report back publicly, every month

What you told me, what I raised, what moved and what did not. Including the things I failed to change, because a representative who only reports wins is not reporting.

Monthly, all year

Part four

If someone asks me this, here is my answer

“Doesn't student council just plan parties?”

Some of it is events, and events matter more than people admit when most of us are here for nine months. But the council also decides where diversity funding goes, sends the vote that represents HGSE across all twelve Harvard schools, and sets the agenda that senior administrators sit down to discuss. I plan to use all three.

“Can you really change anything in one year?”

Not everything. I cannot move tuition, financial aid, admissions or grades, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. What is genuinely reachable is access to opportunities, connection to the other Harvard schools, support for international students, treatment of online students, and how easy it is for a student group to put on an event. Those are the things I am promising, because those are the things the job can actually touch.

“What makes you different from the other candidates?”

I am not asking you to trust that I will listen. I built a place where you can tell me right now, and I will publish what I hear and what came of it. My master's is in data and computational social science, so turning what a community says into something decision-makers act on is the thing I was actually trained to do.

“I'm online. I'm a parent. I'm international. What is in this for me?”

Those three groups are the ones the standard HGSE experience serves worst, and each has its own promise on my platform rather than a mention in a list. I moved to the United States alone as an international student in high school and worked out this system by myself, so the international piece is not a talking point for me.

“Why should I bother voting?”

Turnout in student elections is usually low, which means a small number of votes genuinely decides it. The ballot is in your HGSE email between September 8 and 11 and takes about a minute. Even if you vote for someone else, please vote.
